14 · Embedding bake-off (2026-05-19)

Does a larger BGE help on kb's own design corpus? — yes, but only the jump to bge-large pays off.

TL;DR

Three BGE models indexed kb's docs/research/ corpus (49 HTML files) into parallel kbs on one local daemon. 32 hand-curated queries × 3 modes (hybrid, semantic, keyword) were scored against each kb. In hybrid mode (the production default), bge-large beat bge-small by +15.6pp Recall@1 (0.812 vs 0.656) and +12.6pp MRR. bge-base is essentially a wash vs bge-small on this corpus — Recall@1 identical, MRR +2.1pp. The recommendation is straight to bge-large or stay on bge-small; bge-base is not the answer here.

Setup

  • Corpus: docs/research/ — 49 HTML files (~1.9 MB), kb's own technical English prose.
  • Daemon: a throwaway bench-local daemon on 127.0.0.1:4100, config at bench/kb.local.toml. Production daemon untouched.
  • Models: bge-small-en-v1.5 (384-dim, 127 MB), bge-base-en-v1.5 (768-dim, 440 MB), bge-large-en-v1.5 (1024-dim, 1.34 GB). All MIT-licensed BGE family, all served via kb-embedder subprocesses.
  • Index time: 2.5 min total for all three kbs (i7 Kaby Lake).
  • Modes: hybrid = BM25 + vector via lance RRF (k=60), semantic = cosine on the embedding column, keyword = BM25 only.
  • Queries: 32, hand-curated by reading each doc's title + structure and asking what the operator would actually search for. JSON-lined at bench/queries/kb-docs.jsonl.

What the numbers say

Hybrid: bge-large is the win

Recall@1 jumps from 0.656 → 0.812 (+15.6pp), MRR from 0.749 → 0.875 (+12.6pp), nDCG@10 from 0.779 → 0.868 (+8.9pp). All three production-relevant metrics move together. bge-base barely moves the needle vs small: Recall@1 identical, MRR +2.1pp, nDCG +1.7pp. The meaningful upgrade on this corpus is straight to large, not the intermediate step.

Semantic: where embedding quality lives in isolation

Stripping BM25 out exposes the embedding alone. bge-small Recall@1 is 0.438; bge-large is 0.562 (+12.4pp). Notably, bge-base is slightly worse than bge-small on Recall@1 (0.406 vs 0.438) but better at Recall@5 — the base model finds the right neighborhood but doesn't rank the single best result as crisply. This pattern shows up in published BEIR/MTEB scores for short-prose corpora with low doc counts; bge-base's gains typically appear at scale (>1000 docs, multi-domain) where bge-small's 384-dim space starts to feel crowded.

Hybrid > semantic for every model

BM25 contributes real signal the embedding alone can't match. The lance RRF fusion (k=60) is doing useful work, not just smoothing — every row in the hybrid table is higher than its semantic counterpart by 15–25pp on Recall@1. This validates the design decision in 01 · Storage to keep BM25 in the hybrid pipeline rather than going pure-vector.

Recommendation

For this corpus class (technical English design docs, single domain, ~50 docs), bge-large is worth the storage cost: 1.34 GB on disk plus 4 KB/doc embedding payload (vs 1.5 KB/doc for bge-small). The +15.6pp Recall@1 in hybrid mode is the largest jump in the table.

bge-base is not the answer. If the operator is bandwidth- or memory-constrained, stay on bge-small; otherwise skip directly to large. Spending 440 MB of disk + 768-dim embeddings for a +2.1pp MRR improvement is not a good trade.

A follow-up sweep on larger corpora (a few thousand documents each) is the natural next step. Hypothesis: bge-base pulls ahead of bge-small once the corpus crosses ~1000 docs and the 384-dim space saturates. Until that's measured, this report only covers the kb-docs corpus and similar small technical sets.

Caveats

  • 32 queries × 1 labeller. The numbers are comparative, not absolute. A different labeller's notion of "relevant" would shift Recall ±5pp.
  • Single corpus, 49 docs. Recall@10 saturates near 1.0 across the board; the real discrimination is at Recall@1 and MRR. A larger corpus would let Recall@10 differentiate too.
  • Hand-curated queries lean toward exact-topic phrasings (mostly one doc per query). A real-user query mix would have more vague, multi-doc-relevant queries where the bigger model's semantic reach matters more.
  • kb-docs is heavily lexical — every doc has a strong matching title. BM25 carries a lot of weight here. Corpora with weak titles (synthetic prose, blog-style content) would put more pressure on the embedding model and likely widen the bge-large gap.
  • Hybrid Recall@10 actually drops slightly for the bigger models (1.000 → 0.969). The drop is one query out of 32 — within noise. Bigger models compete with BM25 for slots in the RRF top-10 window, and a small number of queries get a relevant lexical hit displaced by an irrelevant vector hit. Worth watching at scale.

Reproduce

# Download models (~2 GB one-time)
kb model download bge-small-en-v1.5
kb model download bge-base-en-v1.5
kb model download bge-large-en-v1.5

# Boot bench daemon (three parallel kbs against docs/research/)
kb daemon --config bench/kb.local.toml &
# Wait until all three kbs hit 49 docs (~2.5 min)

# Run
kb bench run \
  --queries bench/queries/kb-docs.jsonl \
  --kbs kb-docs-small,kb-docs-base,kb-docs-large \
  --modes hybrid,semantic,keyword \
  --k 1 --k 5 --k 10 \
  --daemon http://127.0.0.1:4100 \
  --output docs/bench/kb-docs-bakeoff-2026-05-19.md \
  --json docs/bench/kb-docs-bakeoff-2026-05-19.json
