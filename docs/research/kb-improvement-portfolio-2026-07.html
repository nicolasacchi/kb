The kb improvement portfolio — verified proposals, rulings, and the road after v0.24 (2026-07)

One hundred twenty reviewer proposals, clustered to twenty-two, each attacked by two independent skeptics. Twelve survived. This volume records what to adopt, what to salvage, what to refuse — and three urgent flags that precede all of it. The evidence lives in the companion reference review (private).

2026-07-05· status:open· project:kb· 120 → 22 → 12 survivors

TL;DR

  • The architecture is sound; nothing warrants a rewrite. Across 178 graded decisions: 79 sound, 38 sound-but-dated, 56 questionable, 5 wrong. The five wrong are specific and fixable — none is structural.
  • Three urgent flags come before any roadmap: the v0.24 overnight run is stalled mid-R2; the R2 cascade policy contradicts the repo's own documented design for history/lists (an operator decision, not a unilateral fix); and the per-Stop capture defect behind the 47 GB outage is fixed only in an unlanded WIP file.
  • The top engineering priority is one small fix: a per-request timeout on the embed IPC read (crates/kb-core/src/embed_ipc.rs:346,437) — today a hung-but-alive embedder wedges every search on that model, forever, across all kbs.
  • The dual-store question is settled for now: keep lancedb + sqlite, harden the seams, and record the swap trigger (~50k real docs; the largest real corpus is ~288). The single-store spike is an option on file, not a project.
  • The public flip changes the security math — fork-PR isolation in CI and a default-config scrub gap for future self-hosters both belong in the Z6 gate, not the backlog.
  • The meta-system passed its own audit: nine of twenty-two proposals died because a skeptic could cite a recorded ruling. The decision-record architecture is kb's real moat — and its maintenance debt (invariant-index bloat, four untagged milestones, a frozen narrative home) is now the cheapest high-leverage work in the repo.

Three urgent flags

U1 · The overnight run is stalled

The v0.24 autonomous run (started 2026-07-04 ~02:00) committed R1 at 12:16, finished writing R2 by ~13:15, and has been idle since — roughly 34 hours at review time, with phases 3–13 unstarted and R2 complete but uncommitted in the working tree alongside the three frozen operator files. Safe resume: decide U2 below, adjust CASCADE_STEPS/SWEEP_TABLES if needed, commit R2 with explicit paths only, then continue the phase sequence.

U2 · R2's cascade policy contradicts the repo's own design record — decide before it lands

As implemented, Full-mode cascade (which fires on every watcher unlink and reconcile-detected vanish, not just explicit deletes) removes history and reading_sections, and list_entries die in both modes; the orphan sweep runs every reconcile tick and would mass-delete pre-R2 rows on its first pass — a silent one-time destructive migration over months of reading activity. The plan text does specify this (operator-locked D3), and the plan's own risk register names the scenario with observability as the accepted mitigation. But the repo's prior record encodes the opposite as deliberate: V0014 (“history is append-only and outlives artifact deletion”), V0015's tombstone-rejoin, invariants #8/#19, the crates/kb-core/src/storage/sqlite.rs:1379 comment, and the purge_kb_data_leaves_lists test. One of these is wrong, and only the operator can say which. My recommendation: history, reading_sections, and list_entries leave CASCADE_STEPS and SWEEP_TABLES entirely (activity log and tombstones are not leaks); if D3 stands instead, the first sweep must be dry-run/loud and T3 must rewrite the contradicted invariants. Either way: the two API delete routes (crates/kb-server/src/routes/artifacts.rs:219 forget, crates/kb-server/src/routes/notes.rs:726 delete-note) were never rewired through the cascade — “systematic delete” currently isn't.

U3 · The capture defect behind the 47 GB outage is fixed only in an unlanded WIP file

The committed kb-capture.sh writes a new file every Stop; one long session accreted 392 captures and 47 GB of lance data, causing the 2026-07-02 fd-exhaustion outage. The one-file-overwrite fix sits uncommitted (frozen by the v0.24 dirty-files protocol). Land it right after v0.24 tags, then dedup the 392-file legacy corpus and retire the newest-capture scoping ceremony that invariant #11 threads through five sqlite reads.

Method

Ultracode workflow wf_775c8c02-acf (2026-07-05, 64 agents, 5.76M subagent tokens, 56 min): 13 Opus subsystem reviewers + 5 Opus whole-system lenses produced 120 raw proposals; an Opus portfolio editor clustered them to 22; each of the 22 was then independently attacked by two Sonnet skeptics — a reality skeptic (does the problem exist in this codebase? does the fix break any of the 35 invariants? is the effort honest? is there a simpler 80% version?) and a fit skeptic (does it collide with a recorded refusal or operator ruling? is the payoff real for one operator? what competes for the effort?). A proposal “survives” only if neither skeptic rejects. Twelve survived; several rejections still yielded salvageable slices, recorded below. Every ruling that follows is mine, made after weighing both verdicts against the full report set.

Effort scale: S <1d · M 1–3d · L 1–2w · XL >2w. Verdict shorthand: endorse endorse-with-changes reject.

Adopted — the engineering core

P02 · Bound every embed/rerank IPC round-trip incremental effort S–M

Reality: endorse-with-changes (high) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (high)

Ruling: adopt — the single highest-value fix in the portfolio. send_recv/send_recv_rerank read the sidecar's stdout with no time bound while holding the shared per-model mutex; a hung-but-alive ORT child therefore wedges all search on that model, across every kb, permanently — and the liveness probe uses try_wait(), which only catches exited children. Both skeptics verified it in code; the handshake path was already hardened for exactly this failure class after a production wedge. Take the reality skeptic's simplification (a lightweight kill-by-pid watchdog rather than duplicating the reader-thread dance per call), size the default timeout for uncapped chunk batches, and add the missing hung-after-handshake test.

P11 · Fix the empty-digest fallback; add minimal parse-health new-capability effort S + M

Reality: endorse-with-changes (high) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (high)

Ruling: adopt, split in two. At crates/kb-core/src/indexer.rs:1153 an empty session digest silently falls through to embedding and FTS-indexing the raw multi-MB JSONL — defeating invariant #11's digest-not-JSONL contract precisely in the format-drift case it exists for (and drift has already happened twice: the sessionId truncation, the AskUserQuestion prose flip that broke ~70/100 decision cards). Ship the deterministic stub substitution immediately with a regression test. The parse-health counter rides the existing doctor/Verdict idiom over columns that already exist; the cc_version pin/registry is cut — no capture-hook version field exists and the evidence doesn't support pre-building one.

P03 · Cap artifact size before read/parse incremental effort S–M

Reality: endorse-with-changes (medium) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (high)

Ruling: adopt, with the corrected framing. Not a public-DoS fix (the API write path already has axum's 2 MB default body limit, and the watcher only ingests operator-controlled directories) — it is filesystem-ingest hardening against the mundane case: one oversized page-save or runaway transcript OOMing the daemon and taking every co-hosted kb with it. The reality skeptic found it's worse than proposed: there are two unconditional full reads (crates/kb-core/src/indexer.rs:867 pre-gate — which runs on every reconcile pass — and :1010). Gate both via a shared stat-first check on a new KbSection.max_artifact_bytes (the attachments-cap pattern), quarantine immediately on first detection.

P22 · Fleet fan-out resilience: compaction semaphore, reconcile jitter, fan-out deadline incremental effort S–M

Reality: endorse-with-changes (high) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (high)

Ruling: adopt, split. All three sub-claims verified: spawn_startup_compact (crates/kb-server/src/lib.rs:924) spawns one unbounded task per kb (a ~20-corpus box IO-storms at boot — the incident's 88% host-IO signature), reconcile intervals phase-align from a serial boot loop, and buffered_join has no per-corpus deadline so one compacting actor drags every federated query to the slowest arm. Ship the semaphore + jitter as-is (S, sequenced after R3 lands since R3 extends the same function); scope the deadline to the search and recall routes with a degraded-corpus signal in the response; file parallel boot separately.

P15 · Pre-public hardening: fork-PR isolation, rc dry-run, attestation incremental effort S–M

Reality: endorse-with-changes (high) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (high)

Ruling: adopt leg 1 as a Z6-gate item; the rest is mostly done or downsized. Every CI job runs its pull_request leg on whatever runner CI is pointed at; the moment the repo flips public, a fork PR executes there. Routing every pull-request leg to GitHub-hosted runners (see GitHub’s own hardening guidance) goes into the Z6-final checklist before “flip public”. The rc dry-run already exists as checklist TODOs — execute it (x86_64 + macOS pre-flip; the aarch64 leg needs public-repo ARM runners, so it smokes immediately post-flip). For signing, skip the cosign ceremony: GitHub's native build-provenance attestation is one workflow line and matches the project's own “provenance passports = theater” ruling.

P20 · Slim the invariant registry simplification effort S–M

Reality: endorse-with-changes (high) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (high)

Ruling: adopt. The always-loaded index is ~3,560 tokens — 59% of CLAUDE.md — and fifteen entries exceed a 40-word budget; #30 alone is 3,003 characters of v0.20→v0.23 UI narration whose full text already exists in architecture-invariants.md, so trimming loses nothing. Scope to entries #28–35 plus #30/#29 (entries 1–27 are already terse; the “#11 is 735 words” claim measured the wrong file — the index line is a fine 148). Make the CI length check warn-only. This work compounds with the U2 aftermath, since #8/#14/#19 need text reconciliation anyway.

Adopted — quality gates and bench-gated experiments

P13 · Build speed: Docker dependency-compile caching only simplification effort S

Reality: endorse-with-changes (high) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (high)

Ruling: adopt lever 2, reject lever 1. The Dockerfile's stub layer only runs cargo fetch — dependencies are downloaded but never compiled into a cacheable layer, so every source change pays the full datafusion+lance graph (~20 min). Fix with a two-pass stub build mirroring release.yml's ORT-isolation split (or cargo-chef). The other lever — pointing just redeploy at the fast profile — was killed by both skeptics: it silently contradicts the recorded CLAUDE.md ruling reserving --release for the binary the daemon runs, on a codepath with a live tail-latency program. If that ruling is ever revisited, it takes a bench and a CLAUDE.md edit, not a build-speed rider.

P14 · Hybrid/embedder end-to-end smoke in CI incremental effort M

Reality: endorse-with-changes (high) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (medium)

Ruling: adopt the 80% slice. Real embedding, real IPC-against-ONNX, and reranking have zero CI coverage — the live-model tests exist but are #[ignore]d, and the e2e suite is keyword-only. Cut the full daemon-spawning HTTP harness (L work, new flakiness axis); instead un-ignore the live-model tests inside the existing embedder job as a scheduled, non-blocking leg, pointing the ~127 MB model download at a persistent cache dir on the runner, plus one small integration: spawn kb-embedder, embed into a scratch lance table, run one hybrid query and one rerank.

P16 · ESLint + react-hooks + jsx-a11y in web CI incremental effort M

Reality: endorse-with-changes (medium) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (medium)

Ruling: adopt, two-step. A 38K-LOC, largely AI-authored SPA runs no linter while carrying nine inert eslint-disable comments and 158 useEffect sites governed by manual discipline — an automated hooks-deps gate is a passive backstop against exactly the stale-closure bug class the invariants worry about. Step 1: flat-config with both rule sets at warn, non-blocking, ignoring web/src/api/generated. Step 2: promote exhaustive-deps to error once the audit commit lands. jsx-a11y stays warn permanently — right tier for a personal tool. Pair with the SPA report's focus-trap fix for role=dialog surfaces.

P21 · Reader-chrome state machine in detail.tsx simplification effort L

Reality: endorse-with-changes (medium) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (medium)

Ruling: adopt for a future SPA milestone, precursor first. detail.tsx is the most-edited SPA file (62+ commits, three consecutive IA re-architectures) with 33 setter call sites hand-synchronizing panel exclusivity across two near-duplicate render branches — invariant #30 literally documents the missed-edit bug class. Do the low-risk extraction first (the ~150-line duplicated right-column JSX both branches carry), then the narrow reducer over {rightColumn, sheetOpen, annotate} only — immersive stays URL-derived per the project's own URL-as-truth convention. Declare the v0.23 chrome the terminal design while at it, and let #30 shrink to its three load-bearing rules (which is P20's job).

P05 · Register BGE-M3, bench-gated on the Italian corpus new-capability effort S

Reality: endorse-with-changes (high) · Fit: endorse-with-changes (medium)

Ruling: adopt as an experiment, not a fix. Both skeptics converged on the same correction: the measured Italian failure (semantic-only R@1 0.583) is the arm the fusion already routes around — hybrid, the actual default, scores 0.792 and ties keyword. So: register BGE-M3 (verified present in fastembed 5.13.4, pairs with the already-registered v2-m3 reranker), run kb bench on corpus-b in semantic and hybrid modes, and adopt only if hybrid beats 0.792 — the project's own “ship only when relevance is at least flat” rule. The MultilingualE5 option is cut: its query/passage prefixes are a separate plumbing project mispriced as free.

The salvage yard — rejected as proposed, one slice worth keeping

P06 · FTS stemming/folding reality: reject

Ruling: the premise was false — and the investigation still paid for itself. Against the pinned lance-index 4.0.0, FtsIndexBuilder::default() already sets stem, ascii-folding, and stop-word removal (English). Salvage two things: a per-kb language config threaded into the one create_index call site, bench-gated on corpus-b; and a fresh flag nobody had raised — stop-word removal is silently ON for the code-heavy corpora, worth its own bench. The “retire the title-boost hack” claim dies: that hack was tuned against an already-stemmed index.

P07 · Gate artifact serve for session/memory corpora fit: reject

Ruling: the headline threat was misread, the small slice is real. Authelia gates the artifact subdomains on kb.example.com too (verified in the deployment docs) — the “world-readable transcripts” framing re-litigates a documented, reviewed ruling. But the reality skeptic found the piece that matters for everyone else: with default config ([outbound] unset), served artifact bytes get zero scrub. After the public flip, self-hosters without an edge gate inherit that silently. Ship the cheap slice: force the session_scrub secrets floor on non-loopback serve of memory-session (and ?raw) bytes, add a boot WARN for a token-less non-loopback bind hosting such corpora, and one sentence in self-host.md. The per-corpus serve="private" flag waits for a real cross-origin auth primitive.

P08 · Scheduled fleet-wide backup both: reject

Ruling: decline — kb backup/restore already exist, and v0.24 scoped this out one commit ago. The salvage is documentation: a systemd-timer recipe wrapping the existing verb with rotation, plus the completeness critic's point nobody else raised — backups outlive kb forget and the delete cascade, so a purged secret persists in old tarballs; the recipe should say so and rotate accordingly. The mid-commit lance caveat can close as its own small fix (enumerate fragments from the open manifest) if it ever bites.

P09 · Write-time near-duplicate guard on kb remember fit: reject

Ruling: decline the mechanism, adopt the prompt line. The fit skeptic is right: kb recall --no-floor is already documented as the dedup oracle, /kb-reflect already reconciles with judgment a cosine threshold cannot fake (duplicate vs same-topic-contradicts), and invariant #10's write-only ingest is a recorded decision. The corpus is write-starved, not duplicate-flooded. Salvage: one recall-before-remember step added to CLAUDE.memory.md, mirroring kb-reflect's own Step 5 — zero code, testable immediately.

P10 · Grow the search eval spine fit: reject

Ruling: agree with both skeptics simultaneously. The reality skeptic is right that the gold set is saturated (Recall@10 ≈ 1.0 — it cannot measure further tuning) and that one named mining source doesn't exist (recollect logs; the queries ring is a 256-entry in-memory buffer, not a log). The fit skeptic is right that building 50–100 labels ahead of demand is speculative. Resolution: search tuning is done until a concrete ranking change wants testing; when one does, build the set to its size. Cheap prep now: snapshot GET /api/kb/{kb}/queries periodically to a file.

P12 · Candidate-fact queue + reflect-debt + scheduled reflect fit: reject

Ruling: decline the daemon-side mining (it resurrects the explicitly-rejected D5 direction and duplicates /kb-reflect); adopt the visibility slice. A reflect-debt count (sessions with memory_count==0 — the exact signal /kb-reflect already sweeps), a one-line SessionStart nudge in kb-wake.sh, and a documented nightly claude -p '/kb-reflect' cron leaving dry-run proposals for morning approval. That S-sized slice attacks the real bottleneck — the dream not being scheduled — without new machinery.

P17 · Query snippets + match highlighting fit: reject

Ruling: re-litigate properly at the next SPA/search milestone — and here is the re-litigation. The prior deferral (the full-search-page plan) was a scoping call, not a principle, and the gap is admitted in the code itself (web/src/components/search/SearchResultCard.tsx: “the server has no match-highlighted snippet”) — any match past character 400 of a longread never highlights at all, while retrieval quality now outruns the ability to show why a hit matched. What changed since the deferral: the reality skeptic designed the contract that removes the original cost concern — a plain-text snippet field under detail=full only, windowed to the real match, highlighted client-side by the existing highlight(); no server HTML, no new escaping surface. Scope to the non-chunked body path (chunking is off everywhere in prod). Honest effort: M–L, because body must join the projection conditionally.

P19 · kb config apply fit: reject

Ruling: agree — this is the already-recorded CLI-hygiene backlog item, not a high-payoff standalone. The gap is real (the CLI prints “restart the daemon or use Settings” while the SPA gets restart-with-rollback), the plumbing exists, and it ships as ConfigAction::Apply whenever the CLI-hygiene bucket clears — alongside the uniform --json error envelope (the actual biggest CLI-vs-agent gap, per the AX review), --daemon name resolution, and the push→events convergence.

P04 · Fix the R2 cascade policy fit: reject

Ruling: superseded by urgent flag U2. The fit skeptic correctly caught that P04-as-written asks an agent to unilaterally reverse an operator-locked decision (D3). The reality skeptic equally correctly verified every technical claim — and sharpened them (the sweep runs every tick, not just first-run; list_entries should leave the registry entirely, not flip modes). Both are right, which is why this graduates from “proposal” to “decision the operator must make before R2 commits”, with my recommendation recorded in U2.

Deferred with trigger

P18 · sqlite-vec/usearch + FTS5 single-store spike both: endorse-with-changes effort XL

Ruling: keep it on file, don't schedule it. The pain is real (126 MB binary, 20-min cold links, the arrow pin freezing two years of upstream fixes, the entire cascade apparatus as two-store consistency tax) — but it is DX pain, not user pain, and the project's own frozen research set a re-spike trigger (~50k docs/kb) that no real corpus approaches (largest: ~288). Recorded triggers for reopening: a real corpus nearing the threshold; lance forcing a breaking re-validation anyway; or build/binary cost actually blocking work. The optional near-term slice — formalizing a StorageBackend trait at the StorageMsg seam — is a fine post-v0.24 testability cleanup, but it touches exactly the files R1–R4 are rewriting, so not before the tag. Meanwhile, do the S-sized dependabot ignore rule for the arrow/lance group and stop the un-mergeable-PR treadmill.

P01 · Storage read-bypass (ReadHandle) fit: reject effort XL

Ruling: it remains the end-state — on the phase-3 plan's own sequencing, not as a standalone rearchitecture. The single read/write lane is the #1 structural tail-latency source and the actor header has carried the TODO since v0.0.1. But the acute incident is ops-fixed, the uncommitted tail-latency plan explicitly sequences the smaller, higher-certainty fixes first (P02 timeout, query-priority lane), and the reality skeptic corrected the scope: a full bypass of the ~120-message surface is XL and the fan-out payoff claim overpromises. Gate the read-bypass on queue-wait evidence from the metrics the L-track just wired, scope it to the doc/search read path, and fight the invariant-budget slot question when it's actually scheduled.

Adopted from the completeness critic

  • Recall-hook timeout (adopt now, trivially small): kb-recall.sh runs on every turn in every project machine-wide with no internal timeout — a degraded daemon silently adds up to 15s per prompt. Wrap the call in timeout ~2s and distinguish “no memories” from “recall timed out” in the injected context. The single best latency-per-line fix available.
  • One second-platform CI leg (default-features build+test) as a portability canary — every current job runs on the same single Linux image while the docs promise macOS/WSL support; the glibc/ORT incident class is exactly what a second platform catches. Full OS matrix: no.
  • Two scheduled cargo-fuzz targets — parser::extract and the session digest parse — the two hand-rolled parsers fed by the least-trusted bytes. Scheduled, not per-push.
  • Record the refusal on multi-human identity: one shared bearer is correct until a second human actually appears; write it into the non-goals so the next reviewer stops asking.
  • Alerting recipe, not alerting feature: document healthz + uptime-kuma (or a systemd OnFailure unit) in self-host.md. The daemon stays pull-only.
  • Backup ⊃ forget note and plugin-marketplace pinning: one paragraph each, in the backup recipe and the packaging checklist respectively.

The strategic fault line

Every deep structural finding in this review traces to one decision: two stores with two transaction boundaries behind one single-writer actor. The cascade engine, the orphan sweep, the compaction babysitter, the ensure-columns ladder beside refinery, the error-string idempotency gates, invariant #1 itself — all of it is the dual store's consistency tax, being paid in perpetuity. And yet the review's verdict is to keep paying it for now: the migration is XL, the quality-parity risk is real, and the scale that would force it does not exist. What matters is that the decision is now explicit and instrumented — triggers recorded, seams hardened, an optional trait boundary sketched — instead of implicit and drifting. This is the difference between a fault line you monitor and one that surprises you.

Finish the reliability arc

v0.24 (supervision, cascade, retention, logging) plus the uncommitted phase-3 tail-latency plan plus this portfolio's P02/P03/P11/P22 are one arc, not three efforts. The reliability lens independently converged on the phase-3 sequencing: bound the embed IPC first (the only failure mode that is permanent rather than slow), then the query-priority lane, then the read-bypass when queue-wait numbers demand it. The remaining observability gap is precise: a slow-query ring and histogram resolution above the 10-second bucket — the exact regime the 2026-07-03 incident lived in, invisible to a bucket whose top reads “>10s”.

The public flip changes the security math

Today's trust model is coherent because the edge is the gate and the operator is the only tenant. Both assumptions break at Z6: fork PRs reach CI (route every pull-request leg to GitHub-hosted runners before the flip), and future self-hosters inherit a default config in which auth-free artifact bytes get zero scrub. The P15 leg-1 toggle, the P07 scrub-floor slice, the boot WARN, and the scrub shape-list refresh (Stripe/GitLab/npm patterns with a dated review anchor) are all small — and they are the difference between “secure for me” and “secure by default”.

Stop tuning search; start showing it

The search-quality program did its job: hybrid is the right default, the reranker and chunking correctly sit off, and the gold set is saturated — which means every further ranking idea is unmeasurable until a concrete change justifies a harder eval set. The user-visible frontier is presentational: query snippets and match highlighting (P17's re-litigation), one filter grammar instead of two, one since vocabulary instead of three. Retrieval already outruns explanation; close that gap first.

Schedule the dream; protect the record

Memory and sessions tell one story from opposite ends. The curated side is write-starved (13 facts vs 196 sessions) and its inert machinery — floors that never fire, pins never used, scope tiers never exercised — is over-built for a corpus this size; the fix is a scheduled nightly reflect, a visible debt counter, and a recall-before-remember habit, all agent-layer, all S-sized. The episodic side has the opposite problem: capture works too well (the per-Stop defect, U3) and the differentiator — digest-not-JSONL with provenance — is undermined by exactly one code path (P11). Protect the record, schedule the dream, and resist every proposal to automate judgment into the daemon; the fit skeptics killed three of those this review, correctly, each time by citing a ruling the project had already written down.

The product frontier: synthesis on rails already laid

The genuinely new capabilities worth building all consume infrastructure that already exists and stays deterministic: topic pages (kb topic <cluster|tag> scaffolding a durable artifact from atlas cluster membership + edges + backlinks, with a slot for agent-written synthesis); resurfacing (kb resurface ranking by inbound-edge importance × attention-staleness from reading progress — the library is currently write-only after first read); and a staleness pair report (KNN neighbors sharing a tag but diverging sharply on mtime — the deterministic seed of the contradiction-surfacing the memory-evolution research asked for). Each has an S/M first slice; none violates the no-in-daemon-LLM line. And two refusals deserve recording so future reviews stop relitigating them: no generic web-clipper, and no multi-human identity until a second human exists.

The meta-system passed its own audit

The most consequential finding of this review is about the process, not the code: nine of twenty-two proposals were rejected because a skeptic could cite a recorded decision — the non-goals constitution, an operator-locked plan, a prior deferral, a CLAUDE.md ruling. That is the decision-record architecture doing exactly what it was built for, at adversarial-review scale. The corollary cuts both ways: the records only work when maintained, and the audit found the maintenance drifting — a ~3,560-token invariant index (P20), four untagged milestones, the narrative home frozen at v0.19, and no recorded rationale for choosing v0.24 over fresh-eyes' Release Zero pick. All S-effort fixes. A system whose reviews are only as sharp as its records should treat record hygiene as engineering, because here it demonstrably is.
