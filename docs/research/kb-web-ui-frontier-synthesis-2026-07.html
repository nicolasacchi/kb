kb web UI frontier: what to change next (13-lens synthesis, July 2026)

Thirteen external-research lenses produced 130 ideas; this is the judgment layer — six programs, three waves, and the refusals that stand.

2026-07-18status:openproject:kb companion: idea atlas (all 130 ideas)

TL;DR

Fourteen offloaded research workers (one SPA source audit + thirteen external lenses: reading apps, spatial canvases, latent-space cartography, spaced repetition, editorial design, quantified reading, power-user keyboarding, 2024–2026 web-platform capabilities, time-as-interface, human↔agent copresence, calm computing, museum “generous interfaces”, mobile ergonomics) produced 130 ideas, catalogued in the companion idea atlas. Three findings dominate:

  • The existing board is validated. The three veins of the July idea board (private) — search evidence, keyboard triage, atlas visual overhaul — were independently re-derived by outside lenses that had never seen it. Build them with more confidence.
  • Six new territories opened. The board mined the SPA against its own daemon; the external lenses mined forty years of other people's interfaces. What came back that the board lacks: the atlas as a selection instrument (Nomic/Projector patterns), a generous overview-first home (museum collections), time as a navigable place (Lifestreams/Memex over kb's unique episodic capture), a deterministic editorial identity (mastheads, errata slips, hash-seeded illuminations), a full keyboard grammar (hints, marks, registers, splits), and calm ambient surfaces (e-ink daycards, radiators) — all legal under the invariants.
  • Five collisions with recorded refusals. TTS, offline packs, print export, read-next footers, and daemon-side media/summaries were each re-proposed by outside lenses and are each already refused or gated. The refusals stand (details) — three independent re-proposals of TTS is a signal worth logging, not a verdict.

Method & honesty

Run 2026-07-18 via two Workflow fan-outs of grok-4.5 offload workers (opencode, researcher agent, variant high): 14 workers in wave one (~748K tokens, 50 min wall), 4 tightened retries (~212K tokens, 9 min) after three workers died fetch-hunting and one lost its structured output. One lens (semantic-atlas) was recovered from its on-disk report.md after the relay failed. Total: ~960K offloaded tokens, 130 ideas across 13 lenses, every lens grounded in named products/essays with source URLs in the catalog.

Program 1 — The atlas becomes an instrument

Sources: semantic-atlas, spatial-canvas, reading-experience, serendipity, editorial, discovery-museum lenses — six of thirteen converged here, the strongest signal in the run.

Today the atlas is a beautiful toggle. The cartography literature (Nomic Atlas, TensorFlow Embedding Projector, Distill's t-SNE essay) says embedding maps earn their keep only when they expose neighborhoods, labels, and selections — and it hands kb a hard rule that fits its explainability culture perfectly: 2-D distances lie, so the UI must refuse to imply them and surface where the projection disagrees with high-dimensional truth.

  • Continent keywords — replace tag-frequency cluster labels with c-TF-IDF over member text: deterministic, decomposable (tf × idf per term on click), no LLM. The BERTopic label path without BERTopic's LLM step.
  • Lasso → working set — freehand-select dots; bottom bar offers open-as-gallery-filter, add-to-list, copy-ids-for-agent. The map stops being a picture and becomes a query.
  • True-neighbors rail + layout-stress badge — inspector lists cosine neighbors from the original embedding space with distance bars; a badge admits “3 of 8 high-D neighbors are far in 2-D”. Distill's lesson, taught in-product.
  • Semantic zoom — ≤6 continents at low zoom, the existing ≤12 clusters mid-zoom, c-TF-IDF bigrams + titles up close; [/] step depth. A basemap, not a scatterplot.
  • Terra incognita — invert the density layer: contours where layout is sparse and embeddings are isolated, labeled with the nearest continents, with a copy-to-clipboard brief so an agent (outside the daemon) can fill the gap.
  • Wild tier: bookmarkable cameras; concept axes (reproject onto an A↔B pole pair through the existing embed worker); temporal cartographic replay (Procrustes-aligned layout snapshots); agent weather fronts (isobands of agent-write mass vs human-curation mass — cartography of the collaboration itself); and the map-home shell, where typing dims non-hits in place and the gallery becomes the list projection of the current map selection.
Why this program firstSix independent lenses, zero invariant risk anywhere in it, and it compounds: labels feed the lobby, lasso feeds lists and boards, neighbors feed the reader rail, and the map-home shell is just the composition of the earlier steps.

Program 2 — The generous home

Sources: discovery-museum, editorial, dashboards, web-platform lenses.

Whitelaw's “Generous Interfaces” names the sin precisely: search is ungenerous — it withholds the collection until you guess a query. Museums solved browsing large heterogeneous collections a decade ago: overview first, multiple representational ways in, never a dead end. kb's gallery currently opens as a filter rail waiting for intent.

  • Corpus lobby — the empty-query gallery shows the collection's shape: census numbers, a segmented category/status bar, a highlights strip of full cards, example searches drawn from real facet values. One click drops into the existing grid, pre-filtered.
  • Portal walls — categories / tags / folders / status as representational tiles (count + sample titles), not text links.
  • Representational cards — cards show a true sample of the body (first meaningful paragraph, heading-outline chips, structure collage) from indexed text, never live-loaded artifact HTML. Grid browsing becomes competitive with search for “I don't know the words yet.”
  • Broadsheet home (editorial lens) — a fifth gallery mode that packs the current filter set into a deterministic front page: lead story by rule (word count × recency × open-comment mass), features, briefs column, a continuing-coverage rail. Same inputs, same page.
  • Issue masthead — per-kb masthead with a deterministic Vol. YYYY · Issue WW from the ISO week and a one-line census. Switching corpora feels like switching publications.
  • Never-empty search — on 0 hits always render: other-mode counts, fleet-scope count, nearest facet chips that would hit, six samples, example queries. Extends the board's zero-result recovery with the museum doctrine.

Program 3 — Time is a place

Sources: timeline-episodic, agent-copresence, dashboards, reading-experience lenses.

kb's most unusual asset is full episodic provenance — every Claude Code session captured and joined to files, commits, artifacts, decisions, comments. No commercial tool has this join. The SPA currently renders it as lists. The Lifestreams/Memex/Rewind tradition says: make time the interaction axis.

  • Artifact biography tab — a vertical timeline in the inspector: generating prompt → sessions that touched it → commits → comments → version diffs, each row deep-linking to the existing surface. Pure join of existing tables; the single highest-value/effort ratio in this program.
  • Temporal search scrubber — filter search results by “read during” a date range or session: what was I researching on July 15?
  • Session trails — save a session's artifact path as a named, replayable, Memex-style trail; a reading list variant ordered by event time with session context inline.
  • Artifact archaeology — first-touch vs last-touch overlaid per section: stale claims (written months ago, never revisited) versus fresh work, from version history alone.
  • Wild tier: corpus time-lapse (watch the corpus grow, scrubber over mtime, atlas coords as stable layout); the session-replay reader — transcript left, artifact right, one shared playhead, the artifact highlighting what was being read at each transcript moment. Deterministic, joins already captured.

Program 4 — The corpus as publication

Sources: editorial, dashboards, ambient lenses.

A corpus of agent-authored HTML looks like a database of identical cards. Publication design is the deterministic fix — no thumbnails, no screenshots, no model calls:

  • Hash-seeded illuminations — a small generative SVG ornament per artifact derived only from content_hash + theme tokens: identical across machines and rebuilds, recolors on theme change. Identicons grown up; cards stop being clones.
  • Folio chrome — an optional reader density: ~66ch serif measure, running header (kb · folder · section), colophon strip (created, hash prefix, session link). Longreads feel published, not “opened in an iframe”.
  • Errata slips — open comments render as a numbered translucent correction sheet pinned to the artifact's top margin; resolving tears a line off live over SSE. The review workflow, dressed in publishing's own metaphor.
  • Galley-proof versions — the diff pane re-skinned as printer's proof marks, hunks grouped by section id instead of raw lines.
  • Year-in-corpus yearbook — kb yearbook --year 2025 emits a self-contained HTML artifact (calendar SVG, growth rings, top hubs, remarkable-days evidence, machine block with every number + query hash) that byte-matches on re-run at the same corpus head. It indexes like any artifact; next year's edition wikilinks it. An agent may narrate it afterwards — outside the daemon.
NoteThe quire-binding idea (reading lists laid out as printable signatures) is catalogued at wildness 5 but collides with the recorded print-export refusal — see collisions.

Program 5 — The keyboard gets a grammar

Sources: keyboard-power, reading-experience, mobile-touch, web-platform lenses.

The board already commits to a keymap registry, roving cursor, and triage mode. The external lenses say: don't stop at shortcuts — steal the grammar that makes editors addressable:

  • Hint mode — f overlays one/two-key hints on every actionable target on any surface (cards, chips, atlas dots, rail rows); F peeks instead of navigating. Vimium's muscle memory over the whole SPA.
  • Marks & registers — ma sets a mark on any artifact/position, backtick jumps; registers yank artifact ids/permalinks/passport stubs ("p = provenance register) and paste them as wikilinks or comment citations. Citation becomes as fast as text editing.
  • Peek everywhere — Alt-hover peek cards for wikilinks, backlinks, session files, memory ids; Shift+F12-style “references” peek listing backlinks + session touches + comment mentions. VS Code's nav model over the knowledge graph, resolved from existing indices.
  • Split panes & workspaces — 2–4 tiling panes (compare two RFCs, artifact + backlinks + session); numbered i3-style workspaces (1:inbox 2:read 3:atlas) with per-workspace layout, filters, and scroll state.
  • Scratchpad drawer — one persistent floating buffer over any view; Cmd+Enter routes contents to note / capture / comment-on-current. Compose without leaving triage.
  • Mobile is the same program — swipe triage on inbox/comments (resolve / defer / reply), a fixed thumb-zone action rail, capture-to-draft queue with deferred tagging. Gestures are the phone's hint mode.

Program 6 — Calm surfaces

Sources: ambient, serendipity, dashboards lenses.

kb refuses push, streaks, and badges. Calm computing (Weiser/Brown) is the design tradition built for exactly that refusal: information as weather — present, glanceable, never demanding.

  • Review mode on resurface — card-at-a-time pull session over the existing queue, showing the reason arithmetic; acting clears (resolve the comment, finish the read) — no “mark done” storage.
  • Antilibrary shelf — a first-class “never opened / zero backlinks” gallery mode framed as research reserve, not backlog; explicitly excluded from debt scoring so it never becomes guilt.
  • On-this-day + session echoes — a date-join facet (created / last-read / worked-on this day ±N years) and compressed past-session beats as resurface cards; bridges episodic memory into the same pull strip.
  • Half-life dial — one slider scaling all decay half-lives, previewed closed-form (“at 90%: ~N items”). FSRS's single-knob lesson without ML fitting.
  • Desk radiator + e-ink daycard — a chromeless /ambient route (large title, one-line thesis, 90–180s crossfade, click opens reader) and kb ambient render --format eink emitting a sparse 1-bit daily card a panel polls on its own schedule. Pull-only by construction.
  • Vital-signs strip — one quiet clickable rollup line (artifacts · median time-to-first-read · open threads); each number deep-links to the surface that defines it.
  • Wild tier: screensaver of unfinished threads (night hours bias to recently-resolved — gentle closure, not open-loop anxiety); corpus weather (hash-of-index-stats → deterministic SVG scene, three plain sentences beneath); walk-by provenance filmstrip of kb why energy.

Cross-cutting — agent copresence, kept honest

Sources: agent-copresence, spatial-canvas, timeline-episodic lenses.

The strongest legal ideas make the human↔agent loop reviewable without giving the daemon a brain:

  • Three-state review verdicts — end a review pass with comment / approve / request-changes as artifact metadata; a readiness panel lists must-fix threads. GitHub's grammar over kb-comments/1.
  • Tribal-knowledge proposal inbox — post-session memory candidates land in a reviewable queue (approve → kb remember with provenance) instead of silent writes. Upgrades the existing distill flow; the human gate is the invariant.
  • Organized knowledge-diff — agent batch edits presented grouped by section and claim type with before/after rendered views, not raw HTML dumps.
  • Agent-proposed layouts — agents emit layout-proposal sidecars (computed outside the daemon); the SPA renders them as a translucent ghost layer the operator accepts, trims, or drags over. Geometry stays operator-owned.
  • Reshaped to stay legal: “CODEOWNERS for domains” becomes routing metadata for agent skills (no identities); “fleet of specialist agents” is agent-layer orchestration whose outputs land as ordinary artifacts/comments; suggest-edit patches must apply via the CLI/agent layer since reanchor stays the only anchor rewrite (invariant #6).

Wave 3 — moonshots (XL, each a milestone)

The yearbook leaves this wave — the catalog rates it L, not XL; schedule it as a December 2026 item regardless of moonshot choice. Default next-quarter moonshot: the session-replay reader — the only item here no competitor can copy (the transcript↔artifact join is kb-exclusive), deterministic and pull-only, and Wave 2's biography tab builds the same joins first. The map-home shell is the conditional alternative: near-free once the Wave 1–2 atlas program lands, promoted on observed atlas usage (standing rule 3), not appetite.

  • Session-replay reader (default candidate) — transcript and artifact under one playhead; the cognitive-flow view no other tool can build because no other tool has the join. No longer from zero (2026-07-20): kb-code's session-diff/1 schema + web-code's SessionDiff.tsx/useSessionDiff are the shipped template for the narrative half — the playhead + artifact-highlight layer is the new work.
  • Map-home shell (conditional on atlas usage) — atlas as the default navigation surface; search dims dots in place; the gallery becomes the list projection of the map selection.
  • Dual-field atlas + memory-palace walks — machine similarity field and operator-placed islands, onion-skinned; ordered loci tours as pure navigation (no streaks).
  • Temporal cartographic replay + corpus time-lapse — Procrustes-stabilized layout history; watch continents form.
  • Split-pane workspaces + provenance registers — the full editor-grade grammar.
  • Multi-facet reflection canvas — four synchronized time tracks (creation, reading, sessions, comments) with a shared brush; saved brushes as named scenes.

Standing rules (added in review)

  • CLI parity for every new noun. The CLI is the protocol and agents are co-users: boards, marks, cameras, trails, working sets and verdicts each ship with a CLI verb or a recorded exemption, so the agent side of the human↔agent loop can read and write the operator's curatorial layer. The board's “an endpoint isn't done until something calls it”, mirrored from the other direction.
  • One container primitive. kb has exactly one durable collection type — kb-list/1, with import/export, SSE, and a golden-pinned grammar. Every new collection-shaped noun (trail, set, board, clip pack, working set) either is a reading list (plus at most a sidecar, e.g. board geometry) or records why not. No third store.
  • Evidence-gated promotion. Wave 2→3 decisions (map-home vs replay, ambient yes/no) are made from observed behavior: surface the already-stored zero-hit queries (endpoint exists, SPA-unused — verified 2026-07-18) and add a local-only, pull-only feature census (atlas opens, resurface clicks, camera recalls) shown in Settings and never leaving the machine. The explainability culture, applied to the roadmap itself.
