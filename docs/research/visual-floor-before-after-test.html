Does the floor work? A before/after test of kb's visual-floor standard

We built the “visual floor” (an AA-locked token + accessibility preamble carried by the kb-artifact skill), then tested it: three artifacts generated free vs three generated floored, same prompt, same model. The floor delivers exactly where the evidence predicted — the accessibility primitives free-form never ships, secondary-text contrast, and cross-artifact consistency — and, honestly, not on primary body-text contrast, which current models already get right.

2026-06-06 ·status:applied ·project:kb ·evaluation · 10 artifacts measured · 6 deep-review agents · 3 repos

Summary & verdict

Verdict — shipped The floor is built and live across three repos (guidance-only, no tooling). Both /deep-review passes returned GO WITH FIXES and every fix was applied. The A/B test shows the floor wins decisively on the three accessibility primitives free-form never ships (skip-link / :focus-visible / prefers-reduced-motion: 0/7 → 3/3), on secondary-text contrast (canon 1/4 AA → 3/3), and on consistency (4 accent hues → 1). It does not improve primary body-text contrast — free-form already passes that — and it converges the accent more than intended.

This is the empirical follow-up to the decision write-up “Standardize or stay free?” (standardizing-claude-generated-frontend-for-kb.html, same corpus), which argued for a thin floor on the evidence. Here we built it and measured whether the argument held. The short answer: the predicted wins reproduced, the predicted non-win (aesthetics) held, and the test surfaced one new trade-off the decision doc only hypothesised (accent monoculture).

What we built (the floor)

The floor is the model both poles of the decision doc converged on: “constrained primitives, free composition.” Standardize a thin, inlinable, AA-locked accessibility + token preamble; leave layout, accent hue, density, and voice free above it. It is guidance, not tooling — no CI gate, no validator change. Concretely:

  • An AA-locked :root token set (light + dark), every text token verified ≥4.5:1 on its background.
  • The accessibility defaults free-form forgets: prefers-reduced-motion, :focus-visible, a real skip-link (markup and CSS), a ≥16 px reading size.
  • The rule above the primitives: interactive elements are real <button>/<a>, never a styled <div>.
  • A scope note de-conflicting kb-artifact (reading documents) from the frontend-design skill (interactive apps/pages) — written as kb's own guard, not a claim about the other skill.

This very document uses the floor — its tokens, skip-link, focus rings, and reduced-motion handling are the thing under test, dogfooded.

How we tested it

A controlled A/B, plus field data, plus an adversarial bracket:

  • One fixed spec. Every generation got the identical task: a technical note (BLAKE3 vs SHA-256) that must contain a primary button, a warning callout, a comparison table, a code block, and two external links, satisfying kb's structural contract.
  • Condition A — FREE (n=3): “style it well, your choice” — no floor, genuinely free (the “let Claude be free” default).
  • Condition B — FLOORED (n=3): same spec, with the floor pasted in (what the updated skill now carries). Same model (Sonnet) held constant across A and B, so any delta is the floor, not the model.
  • Field data (n=4): the frozen corpus/canon/*.html artifacts — older, stronger-model, real-world divergent generations.
  • Objective measurement. A script greps the a11y primitives, counts native <button> vs styled controls, reads the base font size, computes WCAG contrast (resolving var(), hex, rgb(), and oklch()), and tallies accent / theme divergence. A separate luminance helper does the contrast math — no eyeballing.
  • Adversarial bracket. The build was wrapped in /deep-review: four Opus reviewers on the plan before applying, two on the applied diffs after.

What the numbers mean

The floor's biggest win is the layer free-form never touches

Across all seven free + canon artifacts, zero shipped a skip-link, a :focus-visible ring, or prefers-reduced-motion — not by choice, by omission. The floored set ships all three in 3/3. This is the cleanest result in the test, and it is exactly the prediction: these primitives live in the styling layer kb leaves free, and a memoryless generation re-derives them from scratch every time (so, usually, not at all).

Body-text contrast was never the problem; secondary text is

Every artifact in every group passed AA on its primary body text (free 3/3, canon 4/4). The “low contrast is the #1 web failure” literature did not reproduce on body text for a current model — a genuine, honest update to the decision doc's framing. Where free-form does fail is secondary / muted text: the canon passes only 1 of 4, and even the deliberately-curated research house style fails AA on --dim (3.63:1 light, 2.58:1 dark) and --warn (3.65:1). The floor pins all of these by construction.

Consistency went up — arguably too far

Free-form produced 2 distinct accents (canon: 4); the floored set produced exactly 1 — every floored artifact used the default --accent and none overrode it, despite the floor explicitly leaving it free. That is the floor working (recognisable house look) and the “monoculture” risk the decision doc named, now observed in the wild. If per-artifact distinctiveness matters to you, the floor should actively prompt for an accent override rather than ship a sticky default.

The honest shape of the win The floor buys accessibility-by-default and cheap consistency — precisely the two axes the evidence supported with high confidence. It buys nothing on “better-looking,” and it does not even buy much on body-text contrast (already handled). Its value is concentrated, not diffuse. That is a feature: it constrains only what reuse demonstrably improves, and the existing validator can't even see it — composite scores stayed flat at ~60–63, because they measure structure, not accessibility.

The deep-review bracket

The build was bracketed by /deep-review to “find the perfect” version.

Before (4 Opus reviewers on the plan) — all GO WITH FIXES

  • Both skill copies are concurrently active (a personal symlink and the kb-research marketplace plugin) — editing both is necessary, not redundant.
  • The skip-link needs markup, not just CSS. The .skip-link rule is inert without <a class="skip-link"> + <main id> — so the skeleton had to seed both. (The single most important catch.)
  • “Single source of truth” was false: the ~/project/research catch-all corpus inlines its own 649-line house style the floor doesn't reach — reframed honestly and given a precedence rule.
  • The scope note had to be kb's own guard, not a claim about what the frontend-design skill or Anthropic mandate; and the “reserve for playground” clause was dropped (playgrounds use system fonts too).
  • Ship guidance-only: a contrast-check grep would false-positive on legitimate <div class="callout">, and an off-token grep would fight the floor's own free --accent. Defer the validator change.

After (2 Opus reviewers on the diffs) — all GO WITH FIXES, applied

  • Sync drift: the external-link ↗ rule was in the doc block but missing from the skill CSS — added.
  • An overstated figure: callout labels were claimed “≥5.7:1”; the true worst is 4.83:1 (still AA) — corrected.
  • A real regression: the skeleton now always emits skip-link markup, but the research build path's CSS had no .skip-link rule → a permanently-visible link. Fixed by adding the three palette-preserving a11y rules to the research template too.

Honest caveats

  • Small n. Three artifacts per condition; the a11y-primitive result is categorical (0/7 vs 3/3) so it is robust, but the contrast / divergence numbers are directional, not statistical.
  • Model. The controlled A/B used Sonnet (held constant); the canon is older stronger-model output. A different model could shift the “body text already passes” finding — though the missing-primitives result is unlikely to change.
  • Measurement gap. The script keys secondary contrast on a --muted-style token; two free artifacts hard-coded secondary colours it couldn't isolate, so the free “muted-AA” cell is thin. The canon (4/4 detectable) carries that finding.
  • Coverage boundary. The floor reaches the paste-a-<style> skill path and now the research catch-all's a11y; a project corpus with its own house stylesheet (e.g. kb's own docs/research/assets/style.css) keeps its style and is intentionally not re-floored.
  • The floor only helps future artifacts. Already-served, frozen artifacts (the canon) are untouched; this is a forward standard, not a migration.

Recommendation

Keep it. The floor is shipped, verified, and earns its keep on exactly the axes the evidence supported — the accessibility primitives, secondary-text contrast, and cheap consistency — while leaving the aesthetic ceiling free, which is where your “let Claude be free” instinct is correct. The one adjustment worth making: because every floored artifact defaulted to the same accent, if you value per-artifact distinctiveness, nudge the skill to choose a contrast-checked --accent per artifact rather than inherit the default. Otherwise the floor trades Claude's per-artifact divergence for a single house look — coherent, but a monoculture.

Hold Option 3 (a validator contrast/native-element self-check) as deferred: the before-review showed its grep checks are low-ROI for a solo private corpus, and the highest-value structural gap (the skip-link) was better fixed in the skeleton than flagged after the fact.

Open questions

  • Should the floor ship a small contrast-verified accent ramp (pick-one-of-N) to get distinctiveness back without re-introducing per-artifact contrast risk?
  • Is the research house style's --dim / --warn AA failure worth fixing, or are those tokens only used decoratively? (Out of scope here; flagged for a separate call.)
  • Does the “body text already passes AA” result hold across models, or is it Sonnet-specific? A larger sample (more topics, more models) would settle it.
  • Should the kb-artifact validator eventually grow the one check the floor's wins are invisible to — a muted-on-bg contrast warning — once a real artifact regresses?
